> **Quick Answer:** There is no single national time limit. On most interstates and freeways, a disabled car with a driver or passenger nearby can legally sit on the shoulder for a few hours, but a trooper can order it towed immediately if it's a hazard. An unattended car is often flagged within hours and towed within 24 to 48 hours. Rules vary by state, so call for help right away.

## How Long Can a Car Legally Stay on the Highway Shoulder?

A disabled vehicle with someone attending it is generally tolerated for a few hours, while an unattended one can be tagged or towed much faster. No federal rule sets the clock. Each state, and often each highway patrol district, decides.

Here's how it usually works in practice:

- **Occupied and clearly disabled:** Patrol will often leave you alone for a reasonable window while you arrange help. Expect a trooper to stop and check on you.
- **Unattended on an interstate or freeway:** Many states treat this as abandonment. Some tow after 24 hours, others after as little as a few hours, especially in urban corridors.
- **Blocking a lane, on a curve, or on a bridge:** No grace period. Officers can order an immediate tow.

Shoulder width matters too. A car sitting a foot into the travel lane gets moved fast. On a narrow shoulder with no room, the tow can happen before you even finish the phone call.

## What Gets a Car Towed Off the Highway Fastest?

Anything that makes your car a collision risk gets it towed fast, and the biggest triggers are lane obstruction and visibility. Highway patrol prioritizes crash prevention over your convenience.

The usual triggers:

- Any part of the car in a live lane.
- Parked just past a blind curve or crest of a hill.
- Sitting on a bridge or in a tunnel with no shoulder. See [what to do if your car broke down on a bridge](/car-broke-down-on-bridge-what-to-do-safety-steps/).
- Stopped in the left lane or a toll lane. Both are tow-on-sight situations. Our guides cover [the left lane of the highway](/car-broke-down-in-left-lane-of-highway-what-to-do/) and [a toll lane](/car-broke-down-in-toll-lane-what-to-do/).
- No hazards on and no one visible in the car, which looks abandoned.
- Winter storms, when plows need the shoulder clear.

Tell dispatch exactly where you are when you call: highway number, direction, and the nearest mile marker or exit. Troopers can only find you fast if you can describe the spot.

## What Should I Do Right Now to Keep My Car From Being Towed?

Get help moving and make the car look disabled, not abandoned. You can buy yourself time with a few specific actions.

1. **Turn on your hazards** and leave them on. Run them until the battery quits or help arrives.
2. **Pull as far right as possible.** Steer the wheels away from traffic so a hit pushes the car off the road, not into lanes.
3. **Call for help immediately.** Use your roadside plan, your insurer's app, or a local tow company. In an emergency, 911 will route you to highway patrol.

## What Happens If the Highway Patrol Tows My Car?

The patrol calls a rotation tow company, and you get billed for the tow plus storage. You don't pick the company, and the car goes to that company's lot or an impound yard.

Expect these costs to stack up:

- Hookup and tow fee, often higher on a highway than on a side street.
- Daily storage fees that start immediately.
- Possible administrative or release fees.

To get the car back, you'll need proof of ownership, ID, and usually proof of insurance. Call the yard the same day. Every extra day costs you. ## Common Questions

**Q: Can police tow my car immediately if it breaks down on the highway?**
A: Yes, if it's a hazard. A car in a travel lane, on a blind curve, or on a bridge can be towed on the spot. A car safely on a wide shoulder with someone attending it usually gets more time.

**Q: How long before an abandoned car on the highway is towed?**
A: It varies by state and location. Many agencies flag an unattended vehicle within hours and tow it within 24 to 48 hours, sooner in busy urban corridors. Check with the local highway patrol for the actual policy.

**Q: Can I leave my car on the highway shoulder overnight?**
A: It's risky. Overnight is when unattended cars get flagged, tagged, and towed, and a parked car on a dark shoulder is a crash target. Report the location and arrange a tow as early as possible.

**Q: What if I can't afford a tow and my car is stuck on the highway?**
A: Call your insurer or any roadside plan first, since many cover a basic tow. If you have neither, our guide on [what to do when you have no money for a tow truck](/car-broke-down-no-money-for-tow-truck-what-are-my-options/) lists real options before the car gets impounded at a higher cost.

**Q: Will the highway patrol help me if my car breaks down?**
A: Often yes. Troopers commonly stop to check on disabled vehicles, protect the scene, and call a rotation tow if needed. Their priority is clearing the hazard, not repairing your car.
